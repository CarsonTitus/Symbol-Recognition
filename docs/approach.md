# Our Approach

We started by researching symbol-recognition models on Hugging Face. We ruled them out because they were built for clean still images with no background, not a live video feed. Next we tried a YOLO object detector. With our Intel RealSense D455 camera it performed poorly; its stereo cameras are infrared (grayscale) depth sensors, and the color stream did not give enough detail for thin pen lines. We switched to a phone camera. YOLO was very good at recognizing everyday objects like a TV or keyboard but could not identify simple hand-drawn shapes. It has no shape categories, and it shrinks each frame so much that a pen line ends up thinner than a pixel.

So we fine-tuned Google's MobileNetV3-Large, a CNN designed for phones (about 4.2 million parameters with our new final layer). It was pretrained on ImageNet (about 1.2 million photos in 1,000 categories), so it already detects edges, corners and curves. We replaced its last layer with one for our 4 outputs (unknown symbol, triangle, quadrilateral, ellipse) and fine-tuned it on about 68,800 images from five public datasets: Hand-Drawn Shapes, Google's Quick, Draw!, two Kaggle shape datasets (outlines and filled silhouettes), and a set of blank backgrounds. Shapes outside our three categories (stars, hexagons, zigzags) and images with no shape train the unknown-symbol category.

The model reaches 99.4% accuracy on validation drawings and runs live on a Pixel phone at about 18 ms per frame. Phone testing showed its current weakness: cluttered backgrounds and real objects, which it hasn't trained on yet. Our next step is collecting our own photos to fine-tune on.
